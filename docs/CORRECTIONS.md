# Corrections log

A record of things this project first got wrong (or claimed too strongly) and how each was found and
fixed. It exists because a physics project that shows only its final answers hides the checks that
made those answers trustworthy. Entries are ordered by when they were caught, not by importance.

## Physics and numerics

| # | What was wrong | How it was caught | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Kepler solver lost the 2π winding of the mean anomaly, so initial states for planets could sit a whole orbit off. | Round-trip test of `elements_to_state` / `state_to_elements` over many orbits. | Solver now tracks the winding; covered by `tests/test_twobody.py`. |
| 2 | The kernel-per-step design was ~25× slower than a fused time loop. | Profiling before optimising (Phase 17). | The whole time loop runs inside one Taichi kernel; transit detection runs in the same loop. |
| 3 | Test tolerances were first guessed ("looks small"). | Review of every tolerance against a stated expectation. | Replaced by tolerances derived from the method (second-order truncation error, O(dt²)); oscillating and secular energy errors are now separated. |
| 4 | The catalogue semi-major axes are computed with the 4π² shortcut (`a³ = M_star P²` in au, solar masses, years), which omits the planet's mass and disagrees with the IAU-derived G by 3.777×10⁻⁵ in μ. An earlier note called this a "8 minutes" effect. | Re-deriving the numbers instead of trusting the note; comparison against an independent IAS15 (REBOUND) integration. | The mismatch is now stated exactly (`docs/UNITS.md`, generated by `scripts/build_units_doc.py`), and initial states are matched to the *real* transit times by a Levenberg–Marquardt fit rather than trusting catalogue elements. |
| 5 | Osculating and mean transit periods were conflated (planet g differs by 0.3 %). | The first matching attempt could not reproduce g's observed timing. | Matching fits osculating elements to observed transit times; g needs an explicit jitter term, documented as a limitation. |
| 6 | The first inference used a coarse grid of trial orbits. The χ² valley in semi-major axis is only ~0.0005 au wide, so a grid 60× too coarse found a false minimum. | Injection tests at known truth. | A linearised periodogram with two simulations per trial axis, then nonlinear re-fit of the peaks with the full N-body model. |
| 7 | The linearised best peak was sometimes wrong under the full physics; a 40 M⊕ reference mass produced spurious peaks. | Compared linear and nonlinear χ² at each peak. | Reference mass reduced to 5 M⊕; every shortlisted peak is re-scored with the full model (`inference/verify.py`). |
| 8 | Ephemeris removal for gappy, unequally weighted data. | Algebra check against the exact profile χ². | The linear ephemeris is projected out exactly (`observations/ttv.py::profile_chi2`, `inference/likelihood.py`). |
| 9 | Zero-mass Planet X test used an absolute tolerance of 1e-11 (a guess); the measured round-off was 1.04e-11. | Test failure during the final audit. | The tolerance is now stated relative to the signal a 5 M⊕ planet produces (round-off is ~2×10⁻⁸ of it) and below 1e-9 au absolute. |
| 10 | The fitted-period test compared with the catalogue period, which hides a *constant* offset (item 4) inside a supposed O(dt²) effect. | Test failure during the final audit. | The test now differences successive halvings of dt (removing any constant) and requires the ratio 4. |
| 11 | Creating a fresh N-body engine per evaluation crashed the process (Taichi limits field allocations) after ~170 engines, and recompiled kernels each time. | Access-violation crash in the Planet X selection screen. | Engines are pooled and ensemble sizes bucketed (`observations/transits.py`); the screen became ~8× faster. Independent ensemble members make the padding harmless. |

## Claims that were too strong

| # | Claim | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | A cross-check was called "independent" although it shared assumptions with the code under test. | It is described as a consistency check; the independent check is the REBOUND IAS15 comparison (`scripts/validate_against_rebound.py`). |
| 13 | A note said radial-velocity and TTV masses agree. | Not established; removed. Mass uncertainties are stated as they are in the published tables (`docs/KEPLER90_DATA.md`). |
| 14 | Hand-typed numbers in `ARCHITECTURE.md` (a_h) and the units table disagreed with the data. | Numbers are now generated from the reference dataset by scripts, not typed. |
| 15 | "Nothing outstanding" against the phase prompts. | Untrue when said: the demonstration was recorded text, the investigation mode lacked its controls, and there was no profiling or reproducibility check. All are now implemented (see `docs/FINAL_ARCHITECTURE.md`). |
| 16 | A "black window" was suspected in the renderer. | A measurement error: GGUI fills its image buffer only if it is read on every frame. The renderer was drawing correctly. Screenshots are now captured with `capture_every_frame`. |

## Things that remain limitations (not corrected, disclosed)

* The hidden planet selected by the pre-declared criteria is **not** consistent with the real
  Kepler-90 d and e timing data (large Δχ² against the visible-only model). It is a synthetic
  planet, chosen for recoverability, and must never be presented as a candidate for the real star.
* The visible-only model does not reproduce the real g and h timing; g needs a jitter term.
* Eccentricity and mutual inclination of Planet X are weakly constrained by timing alone.
* The look-elsewhere correction uses the linearised statistic's exact noise-only distribution; the
  nonlinear re-fit is not re-calibrated separately.
